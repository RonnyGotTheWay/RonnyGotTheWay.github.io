Overview

This project addresses the lack of public audience voting data in Dancing with the Stars. We built a data-driven framework to estimate audience support from judge scores and weekly elimination outcomes, compare voting integration mechanisms, quantify the effects of contestant characteristics and dance partner pairing, and propose a fairer scoring system.

Decision-Support Focus

The workflow connects interpretable machine learning, leakage-aware validation, statistical comparison, and fairness-oriented scoring design into one reproducible modeling pipeline.

Modeling Pipeline

Module 1

Audience Vote Estimation

Cleaned historical data across 34 seasons and 421 contestants. Constructed weekly features from judge scores, contestant background, partner information, and performance controversy. Used XGBoost to estimate elimination risk and converted it into an audience-support proxy.

Module 2

Voting Rule Comparison

Compared ranking-based and percentage-based vote integration methods. Evaluated prediction accuracy, fan-vote dependence, and elimination consistency, finding that the ranking method performed better in elimination prediction.

Module 3

Contestant and Partner Effect Analysis

Used regression and group comparison to study the effects of age, industry background, fan support, and professional-dancer pairing. Younger contestants, performance-related backgrounds, and effective partner matching were associated with better outcomes.

Module 4

STARS Scoring System

Proposed STARS: Scoring Through Adjusted Ranking System. Combined technical score, audience score, progress score, and dispute adjustment, with dynamic weighting, fairness correction, and protection mechanisms.
